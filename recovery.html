Free recovery workout

Reset your rhythm, one relaxed lap at a time.

A recovery swim should feel noticeably easier than a demanding training session. This workout uses a 50-meter pool to encourage relaxed movement, comfortable breathing, and gentle technique practice without chasing the clock.

Distance: 1,500 meters Pool: 50 meters Level: Comfortable swimmer Intensity: Easy

Your 1,500-Meter Recovery Plan

Complete this session in a 50-meter long-course pool. Each length is 50 meters, so a 100-meter repeat means two lengths. Rest whenever needed and keep the entire workout conversational in effort.

300 m

1. Easy Warm-Up

Swim 6 × 50 meters at a very comfortable pace. Use your preferred stroke, keep your shoulders relaxed, and allow your breathing to settle naturally.

400 m

2. Smooth Freestyle

Swim 4 × 100 meters at an easy effort. Focus on a relaxed recovery of the arms, steady exhalation, and a balanced body position. Rest for 15–30 seconds between repeats or longer if needed.

200 m

3. Gentle Technique Work

Swim 4 × 50 meters, alternating relaxed freestyle with a simple technique focus such as a steady head position or smooth exhalation. Skip drills that create tension or discomfort.

400 m

4. Relaxed Continuous Swimming

Swim 400 meters continuously at an easy, sustainable effort if that feels comfortable. Otherwise, divide the distance into 4 × 100 meters with generous rest. The aim is relaxed movement, not endurance testing.

200 m

5. Cool-Down

Finish with 4 × 50 meters of very easy swimming. Choose a comfortable stroke and gradually wind down the session. Rest between lengths if you need to.

Total distance: 1,500 meters

What Makes a Recovery Swim Different?

The goal is to move gently without adding substantial training stress. A recovery session is not a race against your usual pace or a chance to make up missed intervals.

  • Reduce the intensity. You should finish feeling comfortable rather than exhausted.
  • Prioritize technique. Pay attention to smooth strokes and relaxed movement without forcing your range of motion.
  • Take generous rests. Rest periods are part of the workout, not a sign that you are doing it incorrectly.
  • Adjust the distance. If 1,500 meters feels excessive, complete 800–1,000 meters instead by shortening the main sets.
  • Respond to your body. If you are unusually fatigued, sore, or unwell, rest rather than trying to complete the planned distance.

How to Adapt This Workout

For a shorter recovery session

Swim 200 meters easy, 4 × 50 meters with a relaxed technique focus, 4 × 50 meters of comfortable freestyle, and 100 meters easy to finish. This gives you 700 meters of low-intensity swimming.

For swimmers who prefer variety

Replace some freestyle repeats with an easy stroke you can swim comfortably. If you use a kickboard or other equipment, follow pool rules and avoid anything that increases discomfort.

For a long-course training block

Keep the 50-meter pool format to practice maintaining a relaxed rhythm over longer lengths. You do not need to increase distance every week; consistency and appropriate recovery matter more than a larger number on the workout sheet.

Listen to your body

This session is general recreational guidance, not individualized medical or rehabilitation advice. Do not use swimming to push through an injury or unexplained pain. Stop if you experience dizziness, chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, or feel unwell. Seek appropriate medical guidance when needed, and swim at a facility with lifeguard supervision.
