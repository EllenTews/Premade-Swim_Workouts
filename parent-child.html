Free family swim lesson

Make the first splash a happy one.

Introduce your young child to the water with a gentle, play-based lesson. This 30-minute plan focuses on comfort, supported movement, and simple routines that parents can practice alongside a qualified instructor.

Duration: 30 minutes Suggested age: 6 months–4 years Focus: Water comfort

Your 30-Minute Parent-Child Lesson

Choose a warm, shallow teaching area approved for young children. Move at your child's pace, follow their cues, and repeat activities they enjoy rather than trying to complete every step.

0–5 minutes

1. Hello, Water!

Hold your child securely and allow them to feel the water on their hands and arms. Sing a familiar song, gently splash together, or name the parts of the pool you can see.

Goal: Feel comfortable with the setting
5–10 minutes

2. Reach and Splash

Encourage your child to reach toward a floating toy while you maintain secure support. Let them pat the surface with their hands and notice how the water moves.

Goal: Explore water movement
10–15 minutes

3. Supported Kicking

With your child facing you and their body securely supported, move gently through the water if space and instructor guidance allow. Invite small, playful kicks without forcing a particular position.

Goal: Introduce leg movement
15–20 minutes

4. Follow the Floating Toy

Place a floating toy within easy reach and encourage your child to look toward it and reach while you maintain constant support. Keep the toy close and the activity relaxed.

Goal: Practice reaching and attention
20–25 minutes

5. Water Songs and Routines

Sing a familiar song, practice gentle splashing, and introduce a consistent cue for pausing or leaving the water. Never pour water over your child's face or submerge them as a surprise.

Goal: Build positive associations
25–30 minutes

6. Calm Goodbye

Finish with a quiet song and secure your child before exiting the pool. Wrap them in a towel, offer reassurance, and celebrate their participation rather than measuring progress by completed skills.

Goal: End with a predictable routine

What to Bring

  • A properly fitted swim diaper if required by the facility.
  • A towel and dry clothes for after the lesson.
  • A simple floating toy approved for pool use.
  • Water and any supplies needed for your child's comfort after swimming.

How Parents Can Support Learning

  • Follow your child's lead. A child who needs a break is giving you useful information. Pause, reassure them, and try again only if they are comfortable.
  • Keep instructions simple. Use a few familiar words and repeat the same gentle cues from lesson to lesson.
  • Celebrate small steps. Reaching for a toy or relaxing in your arms can be meaningful progress.
  • Let an instructor guide skill progression. This activity plan is an introduction, not a substitute for formal swimming instruction or water-safety education.

Water safety comes first

Keep your child within immediate reach and maintain constant, attentive supervision whenever they are in or near water. Never rely on flotation toys or swim aids to prevent drowning. Use barriers and follow pool rules. Do not force underwater activities. Children need age-appropriate instruction from qualified professionals, and water comfort is not the same as swimming ability.
