Free beginner workout

Your first triathlon swim starts with one lap.

Build comfort in the pool, practice steady freestyle, and develop the endurance to swim continuously. This introductory session gives new triathletes a clear plan without requiring an advanced swimming background.

Distance: 1,000 meters Time: About 30–40 minutes Level: Beginner

Your 1,000-Meter Swim Plan

Use a 25-meter or 50-meter pool. Rest for about 20–45 seconds between repeats, or longer if needed to regain comfortable breathing. Keep your effort easy to moderate.

200 m

1. Warm-Up

Swim 4 × 50 meters at an easy pace. Focus on relaxed breathing, long strokes, and a comfortable body position.

200 m

2. Technique Practice

Swim 4 × 50 meters, alternating an easy freestyle repeat with a gentle technique-focused repeat. Keep your head steady and exhale underwater.

400 m

3. Endurance Intervals

Swim 4 × 100 meters at a sustainable effort. Aim for a similar pace on each repeat rather than swimming the first one as fast as possible. Rest between repeats.

100 m

4. Steady Swimming

Swim 2 × 50 meters smoothly, practicing a relaxed rhythm that you could maintain for longer distances.

100 m

5. Cool-Down

Swim 2 × 50 meters very easily. Let your breathing settle and finish feeling that you could comfortably do a little more.

Total distance: 1,000 meters

Tips for Your First Triathlon Training Block

  • Build consistency first. Two or three manageable swim sessions per week can help establish a routine, depending on your overall training schedule.
  • Practice pacing. Start at a speed that allows controlled breathing. A steady effort is more useful than sprinting and needing a long recovery.
  • Make rest part of the plan. Add recovery between repeats whenever your breathing or technique becomes difficult to control.
  • Progress gradually. When the workout feels comfortable, add a small amount of distance or shorten your rest slightly—not both at once.
  • Prepare for open water separately. Pool workouts do not fully reproduce waves, visibility, navigation, or swimming near other competitors. Practice open-water skills only in a supervised, suitable environment.

Swim safely

Choose a pool with a lifeguard, follow facility rules, and never swim alone in open water. If you cannot yet swim the full session comfortably, reduce the distance and work with a qualified swim instructor. Stop if you feel dizzy, unusually short of breath, or unwell.
